# Gate 1 annotation-tolerance literature audit

**Checked:** 2026-10-14 09:52 UTC  
**Component:** earliest open gate, independent human-reference reliability for the mirrored 17-frame fixture.  
**Decision:** keep the registered screen unchanged; state precisely what it establishes.

## Local screen and measurement code

The frozen [`score_annotation_agreement.py`](score_annotation_agreement.py) requires complete paired clip/frame keys. It measures categorical agreement for visibility, instance count, and identity separately from center error; center comparisons include only frames where both raters report a visible, single, identity-consistent object. Its registered limits are at least 95% exact categorical agreement, at least 90% unique visible-center coverage, and per-axis p95 center disagreement no greater than 0.5 target-grid cell, pooled and within each blind clip. The CSV parser rejects incomplete, degenerate, and out-of-frame boxes.

For source frames of 160×96 mapped to an 832×480 output and a 52×30 control grid, the 0.5-cell limit corresponds to 1.538 source pixels in x and 1.600 source pixels in y. This unit conversion comes directly from the scorer's source-to-target-to-grid mapping. The known-truth scorer only reads the fixture key after the same pooled/per-clip agreement gate passes; its truth and representation diagnostics are kept separate from rater agreement.

## Primary-source evidence

- The [VOT2014 challenge paper](https://www.votchallenge.net/vot2014/download/vot_2014_paper.pdf) defines a practical-difference threshold to account for annotation noise and equally valid boxes. It estimates this tolerance per sequence, because one dataset-wide threshold is too restrictive and the target plus annotation parameterization affect the error. Its study repeats expert annotations across sampled frames and reports lower variation for rigid targets such as a ball/sphere than for deformable or articulated targets. This supports retaining a target-specific pilot screen, but it also means 0.5 grid cell is not a literature-derived universal noise bound.
- The [VOT2016 challenge paper](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/149502/1/149502.pdf) reports that expert cross-checking and written box rules did not guarantee annotation consistency. Its revised protocol derived boxes from manually or semi-manually refined masks, still visually rejected/reverted 12% of frames with poor masks, and later identified four remaining errors. That workflow is evidence for layered checks, not a replacement for our blinded rater process or truth audit.
- The [official VOT toolkit repository](https://github.com/votchallenge/toolkit) is GPL-3.0 and implements tracking evaluation such as overlap accuracy and robustness. Its current role here is a method reference; no toolkit code or VOT data was copied. VOT overlap metrics do not measure the path-center error used by this project.
- Lampert, Stumpf, and Gançarski's CV study ([TIP 2016 paper; arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.0426)) compares annotator agreement, ground-truth aggregation, and detector evaluation across four vision tasks. They report that detector rank can depend on how multiple labels are aggregated; estimates such as STAPLE/LSML degrade when there are few annotations or high variance, while consensus voting can overemphasize obvious features. This supports preserving the independent blinded CSVs and scoring their agreement before any adjudication. It does not calibrate a center-error tolerance for our clip; keep the 0.5-cell screen unchanged and compare each rater to the separately guarded synthetic truth only after the blind gate. No associated code or data were reused.

## Decision, limits, and next step

Keep the existing threshold and the blind-before-truth ordering so the gate is not changed after seeing labels. Treat the result as a finite-packet process screen for this rigid synthetic sphere, not a population-level reliability estimate or a transferable threshold for generated Wan clips and other objects. When the two CSVs arrive, preserve visibility, identity, box coverage, and center path as separate quantities; report each clip and the pooled p50/p95/max in both grid cells and source pixels; and inspect registered worst frames while blind even if p95 passes. Only then run the guarded truth comparison, including signed center bias and mask-box-versus-projected-center gaps.

The required falsifiable next input remains two independent, complete labels of both mirrored clips. A pass must meet all current pooled and per-clip screens, survive blind worst-frame review, and then pass the truth eligibility checks. The second independently rendered mirrored-seed block remains required before treating the labeling process as repeatable. If the threshold is later extended to another target family or generated-video domain, preregister a repeated-label calibration subset before collecting those labels; the VOT evidence indicates target-specific annotation tolerance can differ.

**Uncertainty and resources:** VOT’s natural-video tracker annotations and Lampert et al.'s four vision tasks are adjacent evidence, not direct evidence about annotating diffusion outputs. VOT’s overlap-based tolerance does not equal our center-path tolerance, and Lampert et al. do not supply a transferable numeric tolerance for this fixture. The local Kubric fixture and exact truth remain the relevant simulator/evaluator for this gate; no code, dataset, simulator render, model, or external software was used. This was a paper/source review only: no compute, inference, GPU, human data, or policy experiment; device and peak VRAM are not applicable.
