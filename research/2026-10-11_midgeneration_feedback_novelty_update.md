# Mid-generation video feedback: novelty boundary update

**Checked:** 2026-10-11  
**Scope:** focused primary-source check of recent work that could collide with the proposed LatentGate residual-feedback claim. This is a screening memo, not an exhaustive systematic review; implementation availability and full reproduction status were not audited.

## Finding

Do not describe LatentGate as the first method for closed-loop video diffusion, mid-generation video correction, or reading motion from video-diffusion latents. Those ideas now have direct or adjacent published preprints. The narrower unresolved question remains empirical: **does an independently measured geometric residual from the current generated sample improve that sample's remaining denoising trajectory, beyond open-loop trajectory guidance and matched fixed, no-op, shuffled-state, and sign-reversed controls?** The current project has not run that policy experiment, so this wording is a research question, not a novelty claim.

## Closest overlaps

| Work | What the primary paper does | Relation to LatentGate |
|---|---|---|
| [CLOVER: Closed-Loop Visuomotor Control with Generative Expectation for Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09016), arXiv v3, 2024-10-16 | Generates video-diffusion visual subgoals, measures discrepancy between observed and planned states in a learned embedding, then uses the residual to refine robot actions and replan. Reports simulation and real-robot evaluation. | Direct collision with broad claims that a video-diffusion plan has never been paired with measured-state feedback. Its feedback changes downstream robot actions between observations; it does not alter denoising steps of the current generated video. It is the clearest precedent for the general loop “generated visual expectation → measured error → correction,” while LatentGate's narrower candidate is “generated intermediate state → geometric residual → change remaining sampler calls.” |
| [SASMA: MLLM-Guided Semantic Correction for Text-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16513), arXiv v1, 2026-08-17 | Decodes predicted-clean intermediate previews, asks a multimodal model to diagnose semantic deviations, then modifies the diffusion trajectory using refined positive/negative text conditions. Its method includes a reverse/dilution step, correction injection, and normal sampling resumption. | Direct collision with any claim of *training-free mid-generation self-correction for text-to-video*. The signal is semantic prompt deviation, not a framewise measured object-center residual, and the correction is prompt/latent guidance rather than a bounded box-mask action. The paper reports additional inference cost: its CogVideoX1.5-5B 1-round setup takes 579.28 s versus 254.43 s standard (2.28×) and peaks at 30.9 versus 13.7 GB on an RTX 3090. That makes equal-compute accounting important; those timings do not predict the cost of LatentGate's different intervention. |
| [Vidarc: Embodied Video Diffusion Model for Closed-loop Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17661), arXiv v1, 2025-12-19 | Uses an autoregressive embodied video model, a masked inverse-dynamics model, KV caching, and repeated real-environment observations re-prefilled into later video predictions. Evaluates action success in simulation and on a real ALOHA robot. | Direct collision with broad claims that video diffusion has not been used for closed-loop robot control. Its loop is across executed chunks and newly observed environment states; LatentGate's candidate loop changes later denoising calls within one offline full-clip generation using a generated-state residual. It is a different control timescale, state source, task, and action channel—not evidence for or against LatentGate's specific intervention. |
| [DreamTraj: Generating 6-DoF Object Trajectories by Reading Unrendered Video Diffusion Latents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00486), arXiv v1, 2026-08-01 | Reads query-key attention tracks and pooled features from an early denoising state of a frozen Wan2.2 image-to-video model; a trained flow-matching Reader maps those features to relative object poses. It predicts trajectories without fully generating or parsing the video. | Direct collision with broad claims of discovering or decoding motion from early video-diffusion representations, and a relevant observer alternative. It predicts a future trajectory from an I2V model and a trained reader; LatentGate's planned observer instead measures the realized state of a generated T2V sample and asks whether its residual can causally improve subsequent denoising. The backbone, conditioning, target, and use of feedback differ. This paper does not validate SAM2 boxes or LatentGate's online observer. |
| [DiTraj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21839), arXiv v1, 2025-09-26 | Applies foreground/background guidance and foreground-only STD-RoPE changes to impose a requested trajectory during DiT video generation. | Closest open-loop actuator and necessary baseline, not the proposed feedback contribution. Full-run target-box shifts entangle early RoPE and foreground routing; the late mask-only actuation test must continue to hold early RoPE fixed, as specified by the component plan. |
| [Trajectory-Level Mode Guidance for Controllable Diffusion-Based Multi-Robot Motion Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36530), arXiv v1, 2026-09-29 | Applies planning-cost gradients and timestep-dependent partial trajectory priors to reconstructed clean robot trajectories during sampling, then maps the guided estimate back into the diffusion process. | Direct prior art for clean-space guidance and test-time diffusion trajectory steering. Do not claim priority for predicted-clean or clean-space trajectory guidance broadly. Its low-dimensional robot costs do not establish an external RGB observer measuring residuals from an in-progress video sample and changing later video denoising calls. |
| [TRACK: Accelerating Video Diffusion via Training-Free Trajectory Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30096), arXiv v1, 2026-09-24 | Calibrates disagreement between compatible large/small video models and routes denoising steps to one model at a time. | Efficiency prior art, not geometry feedback. It needs a compatible paired-model route and does not eliminate decoding/observer cost; it does not change the current Wan 1.3B / CogVideoX-5B-I2V compute plan. |

## Experimental consequences

1. Keep the first claim about **causal, sample-specific geometric feedback**. A fixed box shift, a better open-loop schedule, an MLLM prompt rewrite, a more accurate motion readout, or a downstream robot success rate alone would not establish this claim.
2. Preserve independent human frame labels as the output reference. Do not train/evaluate the observer on the same seed blocks used to claim feedback gain. Report raw initial localization, visibility/identity, path error, apparent size, and background stability separately.
3. Keep the already-preregistered controls: open-loop; exact no-op with matched decode/call budget; fixed action; live residual; shuffled-state residual; sign-reversed residual. A gain that survives state shuffling or is matched by a fixed action is not evidence that the generated state drove the correction.
4. Do not add MLLM semantic correction to the first feedback experiment. It changes the feedback source, guidance channel, compute budget, and failure mode at once. SASMA is a later comparison only if the project asks a separate semantic-correction question and can budget its preview/re-diffusion overhead.
5. Treat a DreamTraj-style learned reader as a later observer baseline, not an immediate dependency. Its reported setup trains a reader and uses a different Wan2.2 I2V backbone; the present gate is designed to establish whether the much simpler human-referenced 2D signal and mask action are measurable first.

## Decision

No experiment order changes. Gate 1 human-reference reliability, independently scored open-loop DiTraj response, separate actuator identification, observer validation, adapter plumbing, then matched feedback efficacy remain the right order. The literature strengthens the reason to keep each mechanism isolated and to state the contribution conditionally until the held-out controls pass.

## Source boundary

This memo checked the cited arXiv versions' abstracts and method/experiment sections available on 2026-10-11 (CLOVER v3; the other listed sources v1). It did not inspect author code, verify benchmark artifacts, reproduce results, or establish conference acceptance. Treat paper-reported numbers above as claims from the cited papers, not independently reproduced results.

## Reconciliation with the Oct 11 primary-source report

The completed **Research LatentGate Methods** report (ChatGPT project conversation `6abec4eb-776c-83ea-a384-cd31853729cc`) reaches the same narrow conclusion: DiTraj is the strongest first **open-loop actuator foundation** because it targets Wan2.1 T2V 1.3B; it does not demonstrate closed-loop control. The testable question remains whether a geometric residual measured from the current sample's intermediate predicted-clean video can improve that same sample's remaining denoising trajectory. No such GPU feedback video or policy gain has been measured. The report says it could not inspect this workspace, so its local-state claims were supplied in the conversation and were not re-executed by that research run.

The report adds three important novelty boundaries:

| Work | Collision to avoid | Remaining distinction for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| [SG-I2V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04989) | Generic zero-shot trajectory control and test-time latent correction in image-to-video generation. | LatentGate would need to show a measured residual from the current generated state changing later sampler controls, beyond a prescribed I2V trajectory or generic latent optimization. |
| [TrajLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00861) | Per-object trajectory control through Gaussian cross-attention heatmaps and identity-aware object tokens. | A new claim cannot rest on object-specific attention maps alone; it must depend on the live, independently measured state residual and pass fixed, shuffled, and sign-reversed controls. |
| [PSIVG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06408) | A generator–external-state–intervention loop for physically corrected video. | Its reconstructed 4D scene and physical simulator provide a different state source and intervention from an intermediate predicted-clean image-plane residual. |

A subsequent targeted September 24–29 screen added clean-space trajectory guidance as direct prior art and TRACK as a separate capacity-routing method. The candidate distinction therefore must stay at the video-specific feedback loop: an external observer measures an RGB geometric residual from a decoded intermediate estimate of the current sample, and that residual changes later denoising calls for the same sample. The new papers do not establish that loop, but the distinction remains a candidate research question rather than an exhaustive novelty claim. See the [focused source comparison](2026-10-11_clean_space_guidance_and_capacity_routing.md).

The report's source-level DiTraj audit also sharpens the implementation ticket: the intervention must persist the active boxes and rebuild the mask used by subsequent denoising calls; a callback that only records a residual is not actuation. Record the exact RoPE start step because the upstream default and local protocol differ. The upstream example's 81-frame setup does not establish compatibility or resource use for the local 17-frame, 30-step FP16 adaptation. Upstream licensing is reported as CC BY-NC 4.0; keep adaptations research-only unless permission is clarified.
