# Feedback-policy collision update and next design decision

**Date:** 2026-10-02  
**Scope:** fixed-camera, single-object image-plane path control in a video diffusion sampling loop.  
**Status:** source review, existing-sample observer screen, and isolated controller mechanics; no new model inference or online latent intervention has run.

## What the wider literature changes

The hypothesis must be narrower than “use feedback to control video generation.” Direct and adjacent collisions are already substantial:

| Work | Established mechanism | Consequence for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| [DiTraj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21839) | Training-free foreground/background box-path guidance and spatial RoPE alignment for video diffusion. | Use as the immediate same-backbone open-loop comparator; box-path control itself is not the contribution. |
| [SG-I2V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04989) | Test-time latent feature alignment to supplied object trajectories during SVD sampling. | Generic intermediate-feature/latent trajectory optimization is prior art. A feedback arm must earn value from the *measured current output-state residual*, not merely add a guidance loss. |
| [DiffTrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17220) | Reads point correspondences from video-DiT query/key attention and applies cross-attention guidance to improve motion consistency. Its official [code](https://github.com/cvlab-kaist/DiffTrack) is Apache-2.0 and documents CogVideoX-2B/5B and HunyuanVideo, not Wan 1.3B. | Strong candidate for a future internal observer/teacher, but its attention-guided motion enhancement is also a direct inference-time comparator. First verify a lightweight observer on the selected Wan path before porting it. |
| [Track4Gen](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Jeong_Track4Gen_Teaching_Video_Diffusion_Models_to_Track_Points_Improves_Video_CVPR_2025_paper.html) | Adds a trainable feature refiner and point-tracking loss to Stable Video Diffusion features, feeding the refined spatial representation back to generation. | Evidence that feature-level tracking supervision can improve generation; a later teacher/distillation path. Its U-Net/SVD architecture is not a drop-in Wan module. |
| [DreamTraj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00486) | Reads 2D/6-DoF motion from early query/key tracks and pooled features of a frozen Wan2.2 I2V backbone, then predicts a trajectory with a trained reader. | Particularly relevant for replacing the red-mask observer with model-internal features. It forecasts an object path; it does not correct the same video sample using its measured path residual. |
| [PSIVG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06408) | Uses a predicted-clean estimate, template/simulator correspondences, and test-time guidance to improve physical video consistency. | Strong collision for predicted-clean guided trajectory correction. Its simulated template/perception pipeline is much heavier than the first 1.3B/T4 experiment. |
| [MotionAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03207), [Self-Refining Video Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18577), [MotionStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01266), [4DStreamCtrl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25479) | Cover post-generation correction, self-refinement, interactive motion control, or causal streaming variants. | Broad self-correction and online-control claims are occupied. The possible distinction is narrower: measure an object’s state from the *current predicted-clean sample* and feed its signed path residual into a later denoising control update in that same sample. This remains a hypothesis, not a novelty claim. |

The source-based conclusion is therefore an engineering test choice, not a claim of novelty: **begin with a bounded target-box residual update inside the existing DiTraj foreground/background spatial guidance**, because it changes the location of the next foreground cross-attention region in the direction indicated by the measured error. It does not directly move the rendered object; monotonic actuation is a hypothesis to measure. Keep adaptive fractional RoPE as a secondary ablation. RoPE strength changes feature alignment, but by itself does not encode a signed left/right correction. Exact source inspection found that DiTraj builds its cross-attention mask once before denoising, creates a fresh `attention_kwargs` object each step, and calls its callback only after the scheduler update. Therefore, an adapter must retain active bbox state in the pipeline scope, rebuild the cross-attention mask when that state changes, and reuse the latest mask on subsequent eligible transformer calls. Keep the baseline's hard RoPE geometry mask fixed. The patch-point sequence and fail-closed capture design are documented in [`ditraj_feedback_adapter_design_v1.md`](ditraj_feedback_adapter_design_v1.md).

## Observer feasibility screen on the saved M0 run

`research/evaluate_predicted_clean_observer.py` compares already saved decoded `x0_hat` frames at steps 4, 14, 24, and 29 against the final RGB frames of that same generated sample. Both use a 9-setting red-threshold ensemble; no model was rerun. At step 4, all 17 frames were scoreable, mean normalized center difference from final RGB was 0.00405 frame diagonals, and mean box IoU was 0.786. At step 14, center difference was 0.00139 and box IoU 0.966. Since the primary action changes box centers while preserving extents, **step 4 is the useful first feedback checkpoint**: it leaves most of the existing 18-step spatial-guidance window available. Step 14 is a second diagnostic/update checkpoint.

This only demonstrates **within-sample measurement consistency**. The preview and reference are from one direction-specific prompt, one seed, and the same red proxy; the final video is not independent truth. It says nothing about whether the object follows a commanded path or whether a feedback update improves a sample. The red-mask stability value is a heuristic, not a calibrated confidence probability. The current `[1,5,1536]` pooled M0 summaries remain unsuitable for localization; a model-internal observer would need spatially retained features or point query/key tracks.

## Proposed causal comparison after the open-loop baseline

1. Keep the current neutral-prompt mirrored-path and shared-noise controls. Score a baseline/no-control arm, foreground/background path guidance, and full DiTraj first.
2. At predeclared predicted-clean checkpoints 4 and optionally 14, decode the exact scheduler-converted guided `x0_hat`, observe all output frames without cropping to target boxes, and compare its measured centers to the commanded centers. Step 4 drives the main update; step 14 can update the final steps 15–17.
3. For the next eligible denoising step, move each spatial-control box by a confidence-gated, bounded proportional residual:

   `box_center_next = target_center + 1[stability >= threshold] * clip(k * (target_center - observed_center), -delta_max, +delta_max)`

   Apply this at the five latent-time samples corresponding to RGB frames `[0, 4, 8, 12, 16]`. Preserve box width/height and lock the original starting box. Missing/ambiguous detections produce zero correction, never a guessed position. The current stability value is only a threshold heuristic, not a probability. Keep the gain and cap fixed before the held-out seed run. Leave DiTraj's hard RoPE geometry and first-three-step collision mask fixed.
4. Compare full DiTraj with fixed zero correction, state feedback, shuffled residuals, sign-reversed residuals, and a fixed-schedule/compute-matched control. Decode previews in all arms at the same checkpoints so VAE work is matched. Keep hard DiTraj RoPE-mask semantics separate from any fractional-RoPE arms.
5. Score output frames with blinded labels that do not use the feedback detector. Keep center ADE/FDE, visibility, identity, duplicate count, box extent, apparent size, and background motion as separate metrics. A path gain accompanied by disappearance, shrinkage, identity change, or camera drift fails.

This feedback module needs a pipeline adaptation so the next denoising iteration reads the updated masks. Before coding that adaptation, prove the baseline and preserve the path/noise/prompt hashes. Do not call an adaptive RoPE-strength result directional position correction.

The pure controller component is in `research/box_residual_feedback.py`. Its six local unit checks pass for signed correction, confidence-gated no-op behavior, gain/offset bounds, start-box lock, box-size preservation, image-boundary clamping, latent-grid rasterization, and a sub-token no-op versus a one-token shift. It accepts normalized observations and returns updated masks; it is not connected to the DiTraj step loop and the tests do not establish a generated-video gain. The pilot's box grid is 52×30 tokens, so every update must log the before/after rasterized masks and quantify the actual token displacement; a sub-token shift may do nothing, while an oversized shift may jump.

## Measurement reliability and statistical unit

- Gate 0's annotation column mismatch is fixed: the counterfactual spec now exactly matches the scorer's CSV, and the shared primary center is explicitly the bounding-box midpoint. The scorer validation reports 16 passing checks; Kubric rendered-mask versus projected-center ADE is below 0.03 frame diagonals in both directions. The two independent annotation copies are still awaiting annotators, so human label reliability remains open.
- Treat a seed block—not a frame—as the independent unit. A five-seed run is a screening study; five same-direction paired wins still have two-sided exact sign probability 0.0625. Extend to at least ten paired seeds before a statistical improvement claim.
- Five seed groups can support descriptive grouped cross-validation and risk/coverage plots, not a reliable calibrated probability or split-conformal interval. Split conformal requires at least 9 independent calibration groups for a nontrivial 90% interval and 19 for 95%. Keep both mirrored conditions, all arms, all frames, and all checkpoints from one seed in one group.
- For the first observer gate, double-label every frame of two seed groups chosen before arm/path unblinding. Report human-human center and identity disagreement; do not use SAM/tracker suggestions as the primary label source. If used secondarily, initialize a separate segmentation session for each predicted-clean checkpoint because those checkpoints are alternate estimates of one sample, not consecutive video observations.

## 2026-10-02 architecture and release audit

The parallel architecture review recommends the current one-read decoded-state controller as the cheapest *causal* first test, while explicitly rejecting broad novelty claims around latent trajectory guidance, predicted-clean correction, attention tracking, self-refinement, or online control. Its exact claim boundary is: **a bounded, single-read predicted-clean object-centroid residual updates future DiTraj spatial masks within the same sampling run**. It changes later cross-attention conditioning, not latent pixels or model weights; whether that changes object motion in the intended direction remains empirical.

The existing eight-step run is mechanism-only: after a step-4 observation, only steps 5–7 remain. Under the pinned 30-step schedule and DiTraj mask window, a step-4 update can affect 13 subsequent transformer calls. Any efficacy run must therefore use the full schedule and compare live residuals against decode-matched zero-update, a fixed action learned on separate data, shuffled residuals, and sign-reversed residuals. No integrated smoke or generation result exists yet.

Release feasibility is uneven. The official [DiffTrack repository](https://github.com/cvlab-kaist/DiffTrack) is Apache-2.0 and documents CogVideoX-2B/5B and HunyuanVideo support, not Wan 1.3B; its Q/K tracking is a promising future observer/teacher but needs a Wan-specific port and validation. The official [SG-I2V repository](https://github.com/Kmcode1/SG-I2V) is MIT-licensed, but its stated tested setup is an RTX A6000 with 48 GB and its evaluation code is still being released, so it is a conceptual comparator rather than the T4 route. [DreamTraj](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00486) is a strong new latent-observer collision: it reads object motion from early Wan2.2 I2V representations, but the current [official repository](https://github.com/whathappen0/DreamTraj) contains the project docs only and says the code and MOVE dataset will be added. It predicts a future trajectory; it does not feed measured error back into the same sample's next denoising calls.

For a later native latent policy, the architecture recommendation is to use independently labeled decoded predictions as a teacher, retain spatially indexed Wan features, first test a linear state probe on held-out seeds/paths, then distill a small temporal observer while holding the action law fixed. Current pooled `[B,F,D]` M0 summaries have lost object location. Gradient-based reward optimization is a poor first T4 step: the runner uses `torch.inference_mode()`, the red threshold is nondifferentiable, and guidance/autograd would require a different memory and UniPC-history design. Full review: [`2026-10-02_policy_architecture_review_agent.md`](2026-10-02_policy_architecture_review_agent.md).

## Connection to robot-training and 4D work

The user's cited **DreamGen** is *DreamGen: Unlocking Generalization in Robot Learning through Video World Models*. Its recipe adapts an image-to-video model to a robot embodiment, generates synthetic task videos, then obtains pseudo-actions with a latent-action model or inverse-dynamics model; its DreamGen Bench studies correlation between video-world-model scores and downstream robot policy performance. This supports the long-term motivation for better video dynamics, but it is a downstream data/policy pipeline rather than a same-sample steering method. [CoRL/PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v305/jang25a.html).

The closest named 4D manipulation reference is **Geometry-aware 4D Video Generation for Robot Manipulation** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01099), [project page](https://robot4dgen.github.io/)): it trains multi-view consistency with cross-view point-map alignment on RGB-D video and extracts robot end-effector trajectories with a 6-DoF pose tracker. The paper's runtime is a separate scale class: its reported generation model has 2.4B parameters and about 47 GB training memory; its robot-policy deployment uses a single RTX 4090. It therefore informs a later multiview/3D phase, not the current 1.3B fixed-camera 2D probe. [OpenReview paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=KoXY5e7bVW).

Other distinct 4D branches should stay in the later-work map: **DreMa / Dream to Manipulate** composes Gaussian-splat scene representations with a physics simulator for robot imitation learning, while **RoboPearls** builds editable manipulation-video simulation from dynamic semantic Gaussians. These contribute scene reconstruction and action-conditioned operators, not the measured 2D residual gate under test. The component order remains: first establish path measurement and causal 2D correction; only then use a simulator or 4D teacher to test depth, contact, and manipulation transfer.
